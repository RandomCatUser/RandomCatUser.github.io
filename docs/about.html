Section One

Executive Summary

This report examines a single, deceptively simple subject: a programmer who codes by day, talks to plants by evening, and answers to a cat by night. Across the following sections we trace the through-line — how curiosity, patience, and a love of small living things translate directly into better software.

The findings suggest that the best engineering cultures borrow from gardening: tend, observe, prune, repeat. The cat, meanwhile, contributes nothing measurable and everything essential.

Key Findings

  • 01 Curiosity beats credentials when the problem is greenfield.
  • 02 A plant on the desk reduces merge-conflict anxiety by an unmeasured but real amount.
  • 03 Cats do not review pull requests, yet somehow improve them.
  • 04 Side-quests (photography, speedrunning, pixel art) are not distractions — they are R&D.
